What the numbers say

Facebook reach fell 65% while engagement nearly tripled

Views dropped from 627K in January to 219K in August. Over the same months the engagement rate moved the opposite way — from a 0.18% floor in April to 0.52% in August, the year's best. Fewer people are being served the page, and those who are respond more.

One post defined the year

The Salem Health affiliation announcement on January 19 reached 30,591 people with 197 reactions and 53 shares — more than double the next post, and entirely unpaid. Community-defining news travels; nothing else in eight months came close.

The top posts by reactions are mostly bought

Juneteenth leads on raw reactions with 534 — but 528 of those came from ads. The same holds for MLK Day (188 of 204 paid), Father's Day, Presidents' Day and Memorial Day. Remove the boost and the organic leaderboard is entirely local substance: the affiliation, the Regence explainer, the Women's Center construction, the Mill City service update.

Family Birth Center is the most efficient account you run

On 4% of the main page's reach it produced 39% as many interactions — a 3.26% engagement rate, roughly ten times the flagship Facebook page. A narrow, high-intent audience beats a broad one on every rate metric.

LinkedIn is growing on zero spend

28,333 impressions, 944 followers, 188 gained this period — with zero sponsored impressions and zero paid follower gains across all eight months. Its 4.4% engagement rate is the highest of any channel, earned organically.

X is dormant and needs a decision

A 40-post sample returned 470 total views and 3 likes — a median of 11 views per post against 1,397 followers. The account also follows more accounts (1,550) than follow it. At roughly three posts a week, that is real production time returning nothing measurable.

Where we go from here

What's working

  • Substantive local news. Affiliation updates, construction milestones, new providers and service-line changes are the only posts clearing 5,000 organic reach.
  • Rising engagement quality on Facebook. Four of the last five months beat the spring floor; August was the year's best rate.
  • Link clicks up 128% against the preceding 243-day period — 49,322 in eight months. People are acting, not only looking.
  • Family Birth Center's focus. A single-topic account with a defined audience out-performs the flagship on every rate metric.
  • LinkedIn's organic base. 4.4% engagement and steady growth with no budget behind it.

What we're working on

  • Arresting the Facebook reach slide. The decline is steady across all eight months, not a one-month algorithm shock — it needs a content answer.
  • Rebalancing the boost budget. Spend concentrates on holiday greetings that buy reactions and little else. Move it behind service-line and access posts.
  • Growing Instagram's scale. Its 1.04% rate triples Facebook's, but it reaches 7% as many people. The rate says the content works; the volume says it isn't being seen.
  • Deciding on X. Revive it with a real strategy, or retire it and reinvest three posts a week.
  • Confirming the August link-click drop. Facebook fell from 5,551 in July to 2,336 in August and Family Birth Center recorded zero — worth verifying this is behaviour, not a tracking break.

Method & limits

Sources. Facebook and Instagram figures come from Meta Business Suite → Insights → Results, read as daily series and summed into calendar months. LinkedIn comes from Page Admin → Analytics (Content and Followers). X figures were read from the public profile timeline. All cover January 1 – August 31, 2026 and were pulled September 10, 2026.

"Views" is Meta's definition — every time content was on screen, including repeat views by the same person and including video and Reels. It is not unique reach. Deduplicated reach for the period was 1.1M for the Facebook page, 139K for Instagram and 46.2K for Family Birth Center; those are the figures to quote for "how many people," while views is the correct denominator for engagement rate.

Engagement rate in the ledger and charts is interactions ÷ views, applied identically to every channel. LinkedIn's own dashboard reports 4.4% because its formula also counts link clicks; both are correct, and the monthly LinkedIn chart uses LinkedIn's definition as labelled.

X coverage is partial. Full January–August data could not be obtained. X Analytics — the owner-only view with true impressions — sits behind X Premium, which this account does not have. The public timeline stops serving posts older than roughly mid-May, and a date-filtered search returned a single result. The X row therefore rests on a 40-post sample spanning May 25 – September 10, using public view counts rather than owner-reported impressions. It is consistent, but it is a sample, and it is labelled as one throughout.

Family Birth Center follower count is reported by Meta as 292 net for the period; summing the daily series gives 300 gross. The ledger uses the gross figure for consistency with the other rows.
