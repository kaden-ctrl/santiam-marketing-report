What this means

  • The staff are the content. A real photograph of people at work is the single most reliable organic performer you have, at 15 median reactions against 4.
  • You are rationing your best format. Real photos are 23% of output. Moving them to half would lift organic engagement without a dollar of spend.
  • Graphics do earn their shares. 2.31 shares per post against 1.35 for photos. Event and announcement creative is built to be forwarded, and that is a real job worth keeping.
  • Provider cards sit in between. A real face in a branded frame lands at 6 median reactions: better than a graphic, well short of a candid.

What we're changing

  • Shift the mix toward the camera. Target roughly half of posts as real photography, drawn from rounds, new hires, procedures, community events.
  • Redirect the boost budget. 64% of graphics get money behind them against 35% of photos. The spend is propping up the weaker format.
  • Keep graphics for what they do best: dates, registration, awareness months, anything where the share is the call to action.
  • Build a capture habit. The constraint is supply, not appetite: someone needs a phone in hand on rounds and at every community event.

Method. Facebook and Instagram post data from Meta Business Suite → Insights → Content, Stories excluded. Each post's creative thumbnail was viewed and hand-sorted into real photograph, provider portrait card, or designed graphic; that classification is our judgement, not a Meta field. All post figures are organic: Meta reports a “from ads” portion for reach and reactions on boosted posts and that portion is subtracted. Channel ledger figures cover the full period and include paid.
